# Clipboard and history

## What is shared

Text with its HTML flavour, images and files. Text and images are applied to the clipboard of the
receiving device, so a copy on one device is a copy on all of them; files are saved to its
downloads folder. Both apps show what arrived in their history.

How an item leaves a device:

- **macOS** - the daemon watches the clipboard of the session. A copy is announced to the online
  Personal devices about a quarter of a second after it happened.
- **Android** - the system does not let an app watch the clipboard in the background, so the phone
  sends when you ask: the quick settings tile `Send clipboard`, the `Send clipboard` action of the
  notification, the button on the main screen, or `Share` in another app. Incoming text and images
  are still written to its clipboard without asking.

What arrives from the other devices is decided on the device that receives; see
[Network policies](policy.md).

An item that Unison itself put into the clipboard is not announced again.

## Sensitive content

Content that the system marks as sensitive is never sent. That covers the markers of password
managers and of macOS (`org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType`, `org.nspasteboard.TransientType`,
`org.nspasteboard.AutoGeneratedType`) and, on Android, a clipboard that is marked as sensitive.

## Pausing

The clipboard synchronization can be paused for a while or until it is turned on again:

```sh
unison pause            # print the state
unison pause 5m
unison pause 1h
unison pause on         # until it is turned off
unison pause off
```

The tray menu of the macOS app and the second quick settings tile on Android offer the same. While
the clipboard is paused, nothing is captured or announced, and an item that arrives from another
device is dropped without being recorded.

## Images and files

Images are sent as PNG; a screenshot keeps its original format as a second flavour when it is small
enough. Images above 24 megapixels, 8192 pixels on a side or 64 MiB are skipped.

A file is announced as its name, size and hash. Copying files in Finder puts them into the
clipboard of the Mac as file references, which is how they travel. A received file lands in the
downloads folder:

| Platform | Folder |
|----------|--------|
| macOS | `~/Downloads/Unison` |
| Android | `Download/Unison` |

Files are fetched in verified chunks, so an interrupted transfer resumes where it stopped. Every
received file is checked against the hash of the announcement.

## Cache and size

An item up to 64 KiB travels inside the announcement. Anything bigger is stored in a local cache
and fetched by the other devices when they need it. The cache keeps at most 2 GiB and drops the
payloads that were used longest ago; a payload larger than the cache is refused, so a file above
2 GiB cannot be sent or received.

The cached payloads and the database live in `~/Library/Application Support/Unison` on macOS and in
the private directory of the app on Android.

## History

Every item that a device captures or receives is recorded in its history, with the device it came
from, the time, the type and the size. The history is shared with the Personal devices: a device
that was away catches up when it connects again, older items first. A large gap is filled over the
following connections.

An item lives for a fixed time from the moment it was captured:

| Type | Lifetime |
|------|----------|
| text | 7 days |
| images | 3 days |
| files | 24 hours |

Expired items are removed every ten minutes and when the daemon starts. Deleting an item removes it
on the other Personal devices as well, so it does not come back from them.

An item that arrived as metadata only is listed as not downloaded yet; the payload is fetched when
you ask for it, which needs the device that has it to be online.

Ways to work with the history:

- the History screen of the apps, with filters by type and by device, copy, download and delete;
- `unison history`, `unison history fetch` and `unison clip get --from`, see
  [Command line](cli.md).

Copying an item from the history writes it to the clipboard of this device and does not announce it
to the others again.

## Holding items until you apply them

A device with the confirmation flag does not apply incoming items. They are recorded in the history
and marked as waiting for you, and the app shows a quiet notification. The item is written to the
clipboard when you press `Apply` in the history or in the notification, and only then.

The flag is described under [Devices and pairing](devices.md#the-confirmation-flag).
